=====================================================

Top ten of the worst things (before)
 1. UWA -> Matilda Bay was a hard cut: campus, a hedge-and-lawn gap, then the river appeared from nowhere; three
    surfaces changed on the boundary line; the far scenery sank and rose.
 2. Matilda Bay -> Kings Park the same: river and red path stopped on the line, a hedge, then the avenue.
 3. The left side of every chunk was one bare olive ramp with identical blobs on it (the scarp): the biggest flat
    thing on screen.
 4. The entry and exit were the generic lawn-hedge-trees clearing (one chunk mirrored).
 5. Only sailing boats under full sail, evenly spread; no moorings (the bay is full of moored yachts).
 6. The far shore was a pale strip with boxes; the river stopped 170 m out and showed a darker second river.
 7. Nothing local besides the boatshed: no Eliza, no bollards along the path.
 8. The boatshed's roof was nearly white (blue-grey in life).
 9. The lawn on the land side was empty between the picnic shelters.
10. Peppermint trees all the same height in a row.

Round 1
 1. Entry: the left side carries the campus on from UWA's exit (campus paving with its brick border, the low
    limestone wall, a last cloister) and hands over to the reserve at z = 22 behind a brick band; the scarp rises
    out of the lawn behind it. The right side starts with the red path, lawn, beach and river exactly as UWA's exit
    leaves them. Shared far scenery with UWA: nothing sinks at this boundary.
 2. Exit: the red path ends inside the chunk (PATH ENDS, bar, stone band at z = 16); the shore swings away behind a
    bank of bush and lemon-scented gums; on the left the scarp comes down to the road behind a limestone retaining
    wall that steps up to 1.8 m. Kings Park's entry starts with that wall (stepping down), gums from the first
    metre and its name wall.
 3. The scarp: a gentle foot, a steep face of dark scrub with sand patches and limestone outcrops, a tree line with
    pale gum trunks on top.
 5. Moored yachts (bare mast, boom with a furled sail, boot stripe, orange buoy) in groups off the shore; fewer
    boats under sail, further out.
 6. The chunks' water now reaches the far shore (280 m); far shore with trees and pale houses, yachts beyond.
 7. Eliza, the diver on her post, off the shore by the boatshed; timber bollards along the path.
 8. Boatshed roof blue-grey (white door between two small white-framed windows was already right).
 9. A tall gum on the land-side lawn in the foreshore and cafe chunks.

Round 2
